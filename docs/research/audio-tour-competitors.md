# Wander: audio-tour competitor research

Research date: 1 November 2026. This is a primary-source desk review, not a hands-on usability test. Product capabilities below are documented or advertised by the provider (or, for Bloomberg Connects, by participating institutions). Coverage, quality, current pricing, and device behavior need testing. A missing feature on a website is not proof that a product lacks it.

## Scope and conclusion

Wander's current MVP is a Westminster map with five recognizable landmarks, an opt-in location dot, a nearby-landmark suggestion, and short sourced English stories that start on tap. Group tracking is explicitly outside the MVP. See [MVP scope](../mvp.md).

Location-triggered audio, self-paced tours, multilingual content, offline downloads, and even route-free discovery already exist. A stronger hypothesis is to serve the moment **“I am standing here; show me what I am looking at and tell me one good story”** with unusually little effort. That is a positioning and execution opportunity to validate, not a claim to have invented a category.

## What could make Wander worth choosing

These are hypotheses and product recommendations, not verified gaps in every competitor.

1. **Recognition before narration.** Solve “which building or ruin is the audio talking about?” Show a clear landmark image, selected map marker, location accuracy, and one visible identifying detail before Play. Generic 3D building extrusions help spatial context but do not necessarily make an attraction recognizable. Test recognition rather than assuming a 3D map solves it.
2. **A satisfying story in one minute.** Make each story independent: identify the place, tell one memorable episode, and point out one detail the visitor can actually see. Additional depth can come later. The promise is a small rewarding moment without committing to a whole walk.
3. **Works for the family member who did not plan the trip.** One shared link, large playback controls, no tour-selection homework. The hackathon version is English-only; multilingual support can be reconsidered later.
4. **Trust that is visible but unobtrusive.** Keep original scripts tied to named sources and provide a transcript. If generative Q&A is added later, distinguish supported facts from uncertain answers. Source links are a trust feature; they become a differentiator only if users notice and value the quality.
5. **A future shared experience.** Group location sharing addresses a separate pain from narration. Keep it outside this MVP and validate whether families value finding each other, synchronized listening, or separate language tracks most. Dora already advertises synchronized listening (linked above). A combination of voluntary family locations, a meeting point and individual language tracks is a hypothesis to investigate, not an established market gap.

Suggested positioning to test: **“Open the map. Find what is in front of you. Hear its story in a minute.”** A credible claim is ease and clarity for a specific situation, rather than “the first location-aware audio guide.”

## Practical comparison session

Start with Waymark in Westminster for the closest match, VoiceMap for a narrated route, and the Rick Steves Roman Forum audio for the original problem. Then inspect Dora's shared-session flow and SmartGuide's browser entry. Use the same task: a visitor opens the product near a landmark without knowing its name, identifies it, starts audio, pauses, and switches language where available.

For today's hackathon, retain the five-landmark journey. Put polish into the identifying photo/detail, the first sentence, spoken delivery and playback recovery. Avoid expanding into itinerary planning, global generation or group infrastructure because competitors have those features. A proposed user test is whether a parent can identify the correct landmark and start the story without help, then remember one interesting fact. This is a validation plan, not a result already measured.

Measure time to first useful audio, number of decisions, confidence in identifying the landmark, recovery when location is inaccurate, and one fact remembered afterwards. Test with the intended family audience. These measures can substantiate “simpler” or “clearer”; marketing pages alone cannot.

Technical lesson from VoiceMap's publisher documentation: GPS accuracy varies in narrow streets and covered areas, and manual locations are useful where triggering is unreliable. Wander's manual selection and explicit location state are worth preserving. [Location guidance](https://docs.voicemap.me/tour-publishers/choosing-locations/).
